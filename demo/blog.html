The perfect suya at home, with a pan and patience

By Chioma Eze · 7 minutes to read

Suya starts with the yaji. Toast groundnuts until they smell like the roadside grills on Allen Avenue, then pound them with ginger, cayenne, a little garlic powder and a stock cube. Make more than you need; it keeps for weeks in a dry jar.

Slice the beef thin, against the grain, while it is still a little frozen. Thread it onto soaked skewers in waves, so the heat reaches every fold. Rub it with groundnut oil, then press on the yaji with your hands.

A cast-iron pan does the job of the grill if you let it get very hot. Two minutes a side, turn once, and brush on more oil and yaji as it goes. Pull it when the edges catch.

Serve it on old newspaper if you want it the Lagos way, with sliced onion, tomato and a last shake of yaji. Cold drink on the side.
